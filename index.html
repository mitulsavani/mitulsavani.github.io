Things I work on:

  • Being close to the problem. I lead each engagement from discovery to deployment, working with the client's teams and business analysts. I architect each process as its own agent, get it through the customer's security review, support it live after go-live, and present the ROI to the client's executives.
  • Production, not demos. At a regional bank, I moved 4 deposit operations processes from manual review to unattended agents: mobile deposit review, merchant remote deposits, duplicate-check investigation and high-dollar transfer-limit approvals. They cover about 1,200 mobile checks a week and 4,500 merchant deposits a month, with risky items still routed to a person. As trust grew, the bank raised the per-check auto-approval limit from $5,000 to $25,000.
  • Configuration over code. I rebuilt a failing C# pipeline (100,000+ documents a week) into a configuration-driven platform that serves 4 clients from one deployment.
  • Working around the platform. When the platform couldn't handle a legal-collections client's logic natively, I built a Python and VBA engine that processes 12,000+ records in about 84 seconds at 99.6% accuracy.
  • Migrations. I built a Python conversion engine that moved 18+ Nintex automation scripts onto HuLoop, so clients could switch without rebuilding each workflow by hand.
  • Turnarounds. I turn around at-risk client accounts with a recovery plan and a timeline. One client went from failing runs to recovered in about 2 weeks, then expanded its automations.
  • Field to product. An SAP proof of concept became a paid SOW. At the client security team's request, I changed our agent to run without admin rights, and that change shipped to every client. I've also written 22 product requirements to cut our builders' debugging and testing time.
  • People. I've helped grow our engineering team from 7 to 24, and I manage and mentor 12+ engineers across the US and India. I want each of them to be able to own a customer end to end.
